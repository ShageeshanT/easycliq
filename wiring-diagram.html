If you've never wired mains before, have an electrician wire the three input screws (L, N, ⏚). It takes five minutes. Everything else on this page is low voltage and safe to touch.

The 24 V power supply

There's no socket for a power cable. The power input is the three left-hand screws. You make your own mains lead by cutting the computer end off a power cord and screwing its three wires in. The four right-hand screws are the 24 V output that runs everything else.

  • Before the first plug-in, set the side switch to 220 V. Sri Lanka is 230 V. If you plug in with the switch on 110 V, the supply is destroyed.
  • L, N, ⏚: mains in. Brown → L, blue → N, green/yellow → ⏚ (earth). The earth wire is not optional: it's what makes the metal case safe.
  • −V −V +V +V: 24 V out. The two −V screws are joined inside the supply, and so are the two +V screws. There are two of each so you can attach more wires.
  • LED lights when the supply is working. V.ADJ trims the output; turn it until the multimeter reads 24.0 V, then leave it.
  • The LM2596 converters have solder pads, not screws, so attaching their four wires needs a soldering iron. Until then, run each ESP32 from a USB cable (laptop or phone charger). The motors don't need the LM2596s.

Where everything sits on the breadboard

Use an 830-point breadboard (MB-102, Rs 250 at Skytronic). The 400-point one is too small for three drivers. Each DRV8825 straddles the centre gap, chip side up, with its VMOT row facing the top rails.

  • Top rails carry 24 V and GND. Bottom rails carry 3.3 V and GND. Put tape on the 24 V rail: a single ESP32 wire pushed into it kills the ESP32.
  • Join the two GND rails with one black jumper at the left end. Every ground in the system has to end up connected.
  • If the red and blue lines on your breadboard have a break in the middle, each rail is split in two. Bridge the halves with a jumper.
  • The capacitor's stripe (−) goes in the GND column, not the VMOT column. Backwards, it can burst.
  • Twist the strands of the red and black 24 AWG wire tightly before pushing them into the rail holes.

  • Connect all three EN pins together and to D21, with one 4.7 kΩ resistor from that line to 3V3. The resistor keeps the motors switched off while the ESP32 starts up.
  • Set Vref to 0.55 V on each driver, with the motor unplugged. Measure between the metal top of the small knob and GND. That limits the motor current to 1.1 A. The ESP32 must be on USB while you do this: a driver whose RST and SLP pins have no 3.3 V is asleep and reads 0 V.
  • Plug and unplug motors only with the power off. Pulling one out while powered destroys the driver instantly.

ESP32-B, the motion board

While testing, it runs on USB from the laptop, so you don't need the VIN wire yet. Tags with a dashed border are for parts you haven't bought; leave those pins empty.

Limit switches

The switch is wired so the pin reads LOW in normal use. When the lever is pressed, or a wire breaks, the pin reads HIGH and the firmware stops the motor. So a snapped cable stops the machine instead of letting the arm drive into a wall.

Link between the two ESP32s (RS485)

  • The MAX485 runs on 5 V, but the ESP32 only takes 3.3 V. The 1 kΩ + 2 kΩ pair on RO cuts the 5 V signal down to 3.3 V before it reaches RX2.
  • Bridge DE and RE on each module with one short jumper, and drive both from D4.
  • Take the 5 V for each module from its own ESP32's VIN pin. That pin carries 5 V whether the board is on USB or on the LM2596.

ESP32-A, the hub board

For now it only has the humidity sensor and its end of the RS485 link. Open the AHT20 box first: it should have four wires (red, black, yellow, white). If it has three, stop and ask; that's a different sensor.

Wire it in this order

  1. Power supply. Side switch to 220 V, cord into L / N / ⏚, plug in, check 24.0 V between −V and +V with the multimeter, unplug.
  2. Breadboard rails. Bridge the two GND rails. With the plug out, run red from a +V screw to the top 24 V rail and black from a −V screw to the top GND rail.
  3. Drivers, power side. Push in the three DRV8825s, add a 100 µF capacitor to each, then the VMOT, GND and logic-GND jumpers.
  4. Logic side. Bridge RST to SLP and run SLP to the 3.3 V rail. Chain the EN pins and add the 4.7 kΩ resistor. Connect STEP, DIR and EN to ESP32-B, and ESP32-B's 3V3 and GND to the bottom rails.
  5. Set Vref. Motors unplugged. Power on the supply and plug ESP32-B into USB: the drivers stay asleep, and read 0 V, until the 3.3 V rail is live. Set each driver to 0.55 V. Power off.
  6. Spin. Power still off: plug in the motors. Power on, upload stepper_test.ino, type a, and all three motors should turn one revolution.
  7. After that: limit switches, then the RS485 link, then the AHT20, then move both ESP32s from USB onto the LM2596s.
